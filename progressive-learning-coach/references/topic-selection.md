# Topic Selection

Choose topics by readiness first, then usefulness.

## Priority Signals

- Foundation: simpler and more prerequisite-heavy topics first.
- Frequency: common ideas before rare ideas.
- Dependency: topics that unlock later learning first.
- Questions: repeated learner questions raise priority for the topic or its prerequisite.
- Carryover: unfinished `pending` and `planned` topics remain visible.
- Readiness: missing prerequisites prevent advanced topics from entering the week.
- Concept density: a topic that needs too many new ideas must be split.

## Prerequisite Handling

Before scheduling a topic, ask:

- What must the learner already know to understand the example?
- Is every surrounding concept already done or intentionally trivial?
- Would the example force two or more new concepts into one lesson?

If prerequisites are missing, schedule the prerequisite and keep the original topic in `backlog`.

## Weekly Size

Default to 3 to 5 topics per full week. Use fewer topics when:

- The remaining week is short.
- The learner has unresolved pending work.
- Topics are difficult.
- Question count shows the learner needs reinforcement.

## Tie Breakers

When two topics are both ready, prefer the one that:

1. Appears in recent questions.
2. Is needed by the next planned topic.
3. Is more common in practical work.
4. Is easier to demonstrate with low concept density.
